# E0088 — GMP arithmetic and Pollard p-1 feasibility

Planned. No authentic corpus or compression result. Rust standalone FFI against GMP on standard 64-bit Linux hosted runner; no new Cargo dependencies. Construct deterministic odd cofactors with a fixed xorshift seed, multiply by prime 65521, whose predecessor 65520 divides lcm(1..16)=720720. Test stage 1 at base 2; require a proper divisor, exact divisibility, and inclusion of the planted factor (the rest of the cofactor need not be prime). Modular multiplication uses two full-width generated residues, followed by GCD, modular exponentiation with exponent 65537, and stage 1 with fixed exponent 720720. Native u128 replay for small controls, plus synthetic fixture checks. Report measured modulus widths, time per operation, peak process RSS and any censored cases.

Widths: 65536, 1048576, 16777216, 67108864 bits. Per case 150-second timeout, 6 GiB virtual memory cap, stop on timeout. No full-target workload authorized by this experiment; the largest case is still much smaller than enwik9. Synthetic success does not predict authentic factors. The exponent bound is deliberately small and the planted prime intentionally easy for p-1. Results are feasibility evidence only.

Prior art: Menezes, van Oorschot, Vanstone, Handbook of Applied Cryptography, §3.2.3 (https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/hac/about/chap3.pdf); GMP integer exponentiation (https://gmplib.org/manual/Integer-Exponentiation). Standard p-1 stage 1 works when a factor's group order divides the exponent and the GCD isolates a proper divisor. No novelty claim.
