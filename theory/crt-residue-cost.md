# C08 — Fixed CRT descriptions

For B=2^k, k>=2, the three moduli B,B-1,B+1 are pairwise coprime (the last two odd numbers differ by two). Product P=B(B^2-1). With k=max(2,ceil((8L+1)/3)), P>=2^(8L) for L>=1, since B^3>=2*2^(8L) and 1-B^-2>=15/16. Thus residues uniquely specify the original L-byte integer, including leading zeros. Let r0,r1,r2 be residues; set t=(r1-r0) mod (B-1), z=r0+B t, u=((r2-z)*(B+2)/2) mod (B+1), where (B+2)/2 is the inverse of 2 modulo B+1. Since B(B-1) ≡ 2 mod (B+1), N=z+B(B-1)u is the unique representative in [0,P).

CRT itself does not reduce information: on the complete residue space [0,P), the three coordinates are independent uniform variables under uniform N. For target range S=256^L<=P and a set of admissible tuples A, probability under uniform target <= |A|/S. For example fixing two residues within R of zero (circular distance) restricts tuple count to <= (2R+1)^2 times the remaining modulus. Requiring two later residues to be within R of the first modulo their respective moduli gives the same upper bound with the first modulus as multiplier. These are counting bounds, not Wikipedia estimates.

IXC1 tests short centered residues or short differences to earlier residues. Because referenced *decoded* values are fixed, field lengths are independent of earlier encoding choices and local minimum choices minimize total field length in this specified grammar. Small residuals can be powerful on constructed inputs; generic residues near k bits each merely redistribute the original information. This construction overlaps multiplicative structure when residues near zero express near divisibility, and rational/repetition when special moduli align with radix patterns. No novelty claim. Further work requires evidence rather than unbounded modulus selection; any selectable moduli/parameters must be charged.
