# C09 — Reversible modular difference transformations

Let B=256 and N=sum_{i=0}^{L-1} a_i B^i. For lag d>=1 define T_d(N)=(1-B^d)N mod B^L. Since 1-B^d is odd, it is a unit modulo B^L. More explicitly its inverse is the finite geometric series sum_{j=0}^{ceil(L/d)-1} B^(jd), as their product is 1-B^(d ceil(L/d)) ≡ 1. Repeating the transform r times raises the multiplier to power r and inverse to power r. The digit algorithm computes subtraction from least significant to most significant, with signed borrow; inverse performs addition of already recovered delayed digits with carry. This is distinct from a digitwise difference over Z/256Z, which suppresses inter-digit carries, or XOR difference over F_2^8. All three are bijections and preserve the count of possible L-byte inputs; uniform inputs remain uniform, so no universal compression gain follows.

A structured integer close to a finite geometric expansion can become sparse after multiplication by (1-B^d). Digitwise differences can expose constant increments; XOR exposes repeated binary patterns. Higher powers are finite differences with binomial coefficients in their respective algebra. Actual compressed sizes depend on downstream code, not zero fraction or empirical entropy alone. Compare identity and every charged selector with the same downstream backend. Choosing among 28 transforms costs one byte in IXT1; transformed frame must be fully reconstructed, and whole-stream gzip results are hybrid controls, not pure arithmetic records. No novelty claim; finite differences, modular arithmetic and predictive coding are established.
