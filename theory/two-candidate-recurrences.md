# Two-candidate arithmetic recurrences

At a fixed decoded context, the previous occurrence need not be the best recurrence seed: the second-most-recent occurrence can have a longer matching continuation. Store two tagged endpoints per context. Their distances are computable from the decoded prefix and the fixed table; on collision, discard both old endpoints rather than merge unrelated keys. Since the second endpoint is older than the first, if the newest is stale then the second is stale too. Selecting between two available recurrence seeds requires a charged binary decision, but never an explicit distance. Its instantaneous ideal cost is -log2 P(selector | last digit). This increases potential recurrence coverage and may reduce static root decisions, at the cost of selector bits, extra 8 MiB memory and longer search. With one valid candidate, there is no selector cost. Match search is encoder-only and compares actual digits, including overlap. The selected distance is used by the already verified adaptive length model. Both sides maintain all history digits irrespective of parse. This is a limited LZP candidate set, not an optimal search or new theorem. E0060 compares length16, dual16, dual32 on the same authentic regions, reporting alternate selections and full charged archives. No gain presumed.
